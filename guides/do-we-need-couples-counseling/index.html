Do we need couples counseling? 8 signs

You don't have to be on the edge of a breakup to get help. Here's how to tell when couples counseling is the right call, what it actually is, and what you can try on your own in the meantime.

Updated 29 October 2026By Utku, founder of Deesca7 min read
Short answer

Consider couples counseling if the same problem has lasted for months despite trying, if conversations turn into criticism or contempt, if one of you has emotionally checked out, after a betrayal, around a big life change, or if either of you is thinking about leaving. If there's fear, threats or violence, get help right away. For everyday arguments that you can still talk about, there's a lot you can try on your own first.

8 signs it's time

  1. The same fight, for months. You've talked about it many times and nothing changes.
  2. You can't talk without it escalating. Small topics turn into big fights almost every time.
  3. Criticism and contempt. Eye-rolling, sarcasm, name-calling, or talking about each other's character instead of what happened.
  4. One of you has checked out. Shrugging, stonewalling, or "I don't care anymore."
  5. Trust has been broken. Cheating, lying, or hidden money, and you both want to try to rebuild.
  6. A big change is straining you. A baby, a move, illness, job loss, or caring for a parent.
  7. You live like roommates. Little affection, little curiosity about each other, and no idea how to get it back.
  8. Someone is thinking about leaving. Counseling can help you decide together, whichever way it goes.

If there's fear in your relationship, such as threats, control or any violence, joint counseling is often not recommended as a first step. Please contact local emergency services or a domestic abuse helpline, and consider individual support first.

Couples counseling vs couples therapy

The two terms are often used interchangeably, and in many places they describe the same thing. When people do draw a line, couples counseling tends to mean shorter, more practical work on a specific problem, like communication or a recurring conflict, while couples therapy can go deeper into long-standing patterns and each person's history. Marriage counseling is simply couples counseling for married couples, relationship counseling is another name for the same help, and you don't need to be married to go.

What matters more than the label is the person: look for a licensed professional with specific training in working with couples.

What counseling is actually like

Usually you meet together, in person or online, for around an hour at a time. The counselor helps each of you say what you need, slows down the moments that usually escalate, and gives you things to practise between sessions. It isn't about deciding who's to blame, and a good counselor won't take sides.

How to find a couples counselor

  • Look for someone licensed in your country who lists couples work as a specialty.
  • Ask about their approach. Methods such as the Gottman Method or Emotionally Focused Therapy are designed specifically for couples.
  • Check whether online sessions are available if schedules or distance make in-person sessions hard.
  • It's normal to try a first session and decide whether the fit feels right for both of you.

What to try on your own first

If you can still talk about your problems, even if it's hard, these often help, and they're useful alongside counseling too:

  • Pause before it escalates. Agree a signal for "we need twenty minutes," and always say when you'll come back.
  • One topic at a time, at a calm moment, not at midnight.
  • Find the need under the topic. "When you didn't text, I felt like an afterthought" lands better than "you never text."
  • Repeat back before you answer, until the other person says "yes, that's it."
  • End with one small step, and a day to check in.

Common questions

Does couples counseling work?

Research generally finds that couples counseling helps many couples, especially when they start before problems become entrenched. It works best when both partners take part willingly.

What's the difference between couples counseling and couples therapy?

The terms are often used for the same thing. Where a difference is made, counseling is shorter and focused on a specific problem, while therapy goes deeper into long-standing patterns.

Can we go to couples counseling if we're not married?

Yes. Couples counseling is for any couple, whether you're dating, living together, engaged or married.

What if my partner won't go to counseling?

Individual counseling can still help you understand the pattern and change your part of it. You can also invite them again later, framed as something for both of you rather than a judgement about them.

Can an app replace couples counseling?

No. An app like Deesca can help with everyday conflict and communication, but it isn't a trained professional. Use counseling for long-lasting problems, betrayal or anything involving fear.
