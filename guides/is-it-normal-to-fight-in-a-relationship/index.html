Is it normal to fight in a relationship?

You love each other, and you still argue. Maybe more than you'd like. Here's what's normal, what isn't, and how to tell the difference.

Updated 2 November 2026By Utku, founder of Deesca6 min read
Short answer

Yes, fighting is normal in a relationship. Two people with different needs will disagree, and couples who never argue are often avoiding things. What matters isn't how often you fight but how: whether you stay respectful, whether you repair afterwards, and whether anything gets resolved. Fights with contempt, threats, fear or no repair are not normal, however rare they are.

How often do couples fight?

There's no healthy number. Some happy couples bicker most days and laugh it off; others rarely argue but talk things through quietly. Researchers who study couples have found that style matters less than balance: in stable relationships, positive moments during conflict, like humour, affection or agreement, clearly outweigh the negative ones.

A better question than "how often?" is: after we argue, do we feel closer, or further apart?

Is it normal to fight every day?

Small daily friction, like who forgot the bins, is common, especially during stressful times: a new baby, moving, money worries, long distance or a new job. Real arguments every day are a sign something needs attention. Usually it's one or two unresolved issues showing up in different forms. Why the same argument keeps coming back.

Is it normal to fight early in a relationship?

Some conflict in the first months is normal and even useful: it's how you learn how each other handles disagreement. Watch how it's handled more than the fact it happens. Respect, listening and repair early on are good signs. Contempt, jealousy that controls, or making you feel small are not.

Signs it's time to worry

  • You're afraid of how they'll react, or you walk on eggshells.
  • There's name-calling, mocking, threats, or any physical aggression.
  • Fights end in long silences used as punishment. More on the silent treatment.
  • You've stopped bringing things up to keep the peace.
  • You're told your memory of events is wrong, again and again.
  • Nothing ever gets resolved, however you both try.

If you don't feel safe, please reach out to local emergency services or a domestic abuse helpline. A couples app or exercise is not the right tool for that situation.

How to fight better

  1. Agree a few rules for fighting fair on a calm day.
  2. Take a twenty-minute break when either of you is flooded.
  3. Talk about the need under the topic, not just the topic.
  4. Repair afterwards: a real apology, a hug, a plan. Questions to ask after a fight.
  5. If it keeps getting worse, consider a couples counselor. 8 signs it's time.

Common questions

Is it normal to fight in a relationship?

Yes. Disagreement is part of any close relationship. What matters is how you fight: respectfully, with breaks when needed, and with repair afterwards.

How often is it normal to argue with your partner?

There's no healthy number. Some couples bicker often and stay happy. The warning signs are fights that are cruel, frightening, or never resolved.

Is it normal to fight with your partner every day?

Daily minor friction can happen during stressful periods, but real arguments every day usually mean an unresolved issue that needs a proper conversation.

Is fighting healthy in a relationship?

Respectful conflict can be healthy: it brings problems into the open and helps you understand each other. Contempt, threats and fear are never healthy.
