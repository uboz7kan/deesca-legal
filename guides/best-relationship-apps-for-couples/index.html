Best relationship apps for couples (2026)

Seven apps couples actually use, compared by what each one is for: staying close day to day, learning skills together, or getting through an argument. Including which are free, which use AI, and when an app is not the right tool.

Updated 2 November 2026By Utku, founder of Deesca10 min read
Short answer

There's no single best relationship app, because they do different jobs. If you keep having the same argument, Deesca is the one built for it: both sides go in, your partner can add theirs privately, and you get back what each of you needs and one next step, with no winner. For staying connected every day, Paired and Flamme are the most popular. For a structured programme, Lasting. For talking things through with an AI by voice, Maia. For free conversation prompts, Gottman Card Decks and Love Nudge.

A note on bias: we make Deesca, which is on this list. Nobody paid to be included, and we've described every app from its own App Store listing and website, checked in November 2026. We've tried to be as clear about where Deesca is not the right pick as we are about everyone else.

02Paired

Best for: a daily habit of staying close

Paired is one of the best-known couples apps. You and your partner link accounts and answer a short daily question; your partner's answer unlocks once you've both replied, which gives you something to talk about. Premium adds guided journeys, quizzes, games and topics such as money and love languages. It's popular with long-distance couples.

Good to know
On iPhone and Android. Both partners use the app together.
Consider
It's built for connection, not for the middle of a fight. Most of the content is in the paid tier.

03Lasting

Best for: a structured programme to work through together

Lasting offers guided sessions where each partner reflects on their own, then you discuss together. The library covers communication, conflict, repair, trust, money, intimacy and more. A handful of foundation sessions are free; a subscription unlocks the rest for two people. It comes from the company behind Talkspace.

Good to know
Self-guided: you work through lessons at your own pace.
Consider
It's a course, so it's better for building skills over weeks than for help with tonight's argument.

04Maia

Best for: talking things through with an AI, any time

Maia is an AI couples support app with chat and voice conversations, available around the clock. It also has quizzes, games, goal-setting, date suggestions and reminders for milestones, and it offers help with resolving disagreements.

Good to know
Voice-guided sessions suit people who'd rather talk than type.
Consider
Check which features need the premium tier before you rely on them.

05Flamme

Best for: playful rituals with some AI on top

Flamme is a cosy couples app built around daily questions, quizzes, mood check-ins, a shared bucket list and home-screen widgets for anniversaries and long-distance countdowns. Premium adds an AI relationship coach that can help with gifts, dates and what to say after an argument.

Good to know
Both partners pair their accounts; several features depend on both of you using it.
Consider
The AI coach and many extras are premium. It's mainly a connection app, not a mediator.

06Gottman Card Decks

Best for: free, research-based conversation starters

From the Gottman Institute, this free app has 22 card decks with more than 1,000 questions, prompts and ideas, from getting to know each other better to date nights and intimacy. You can favourite the cards you like.

Good to know
Completely free, no account needed to start.
Consider
It's a deck of prompts, not an interactive coach: it won't help you untangle a specific argument.

07Love Nudge

Best for: couples who think in love languages

The official app from The 5 Love Languages. You take the love-language quiz, then send each other playful nudges, set small goals and keep track of how you're both doing.

Good to know
Free on iPhone and Android.
Consider
It's simple by design and needs an internet connection. It's about small gestures, not conflict.

When an app isn't enough

Two other options come up in almost every conversation about relationship apps:

ChatGPT and other chatbots

Free and thoughtful, but it only hears the side you type and tends to agree with the person asking. If you use one, write your partner's side the way they'd tell it. More on AI relationship advice.

A couples therapist

For long-lasting problems, betrayal, mental health struggles, or anything involving fear, a trained professional is the right call. No app is therapy. 8 signs you need counseling.

If you don't feel safe, please don't use an app to work it out. Contact local emergency services or a qualified professional.

How to choose

  1. Name the job. Staying close, learning skills, or getting through a specific argument? Pick the app built for that job, not the one with the longest feature list.
  2. Check whether your partner is in it. Some apps need both of you; some let one person start alone. Choose what fits how willing your partner is right now.
  3. Read the privacy answers. Can your partner see what you write? Is your content used to train AI or sold? Can you delete everything yourself?
  4. Try the free part first. Every app here can be tried without paying. Use it for a week before you subscribe.
  5. Use it together. An app works best as a starting point for a real conversation, not a replacement for one.

Common questions

What is the best relationship app for couples?

It depends on what you need. Paired and Flamme are the most popular for daily connection, Lasting for a structured programme, Maia for AI conversations, Gottman Card Decks and Love Nudge for free prompts, and Deesca for working through an argument you keep having.

Are there free relationship apps for couples?

Yes. Gottman Card Decks and Love Nudge are free. Paired, Maia, Flamme and Lasting are free to download with optional subscriptions, and Deesca is free to try on iPhone.

Which relationship app helps with arguments?

Deesca is built specifically for arguments: both sides go in and you get a shared plan without a winner. Maia and Flamme's premium coach also offer help with disagreements, and Lasting has guided sessions on conflict and repair.

Do both partners need the app?

Paired, Flamme and Lasting are designed for both partners. Deesca works alone or together: you can describe both sides yourself, or invite your partner into a private shared room.

Can a relationship app replace couples therapy?

No. Apps can help with everyday connection and conflict, but therapy is a relationship with a trained professional. For long-lasting problems or anything involving fear, see a couples therapist.
