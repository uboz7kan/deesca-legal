What to say after a fight with your partner

The fight is over, the silence isn't. Here's how to start the conversation that comes next, in person or over text, and the lines that quietly start the fight again.

Updated 29 October 2026By Utku, founder of Deesca7 min read
Short answer

Wait until you're both calm, then open with something that lowers the temperature instead of reopening the case. A good repair has three parts: acknowledge how they felt, own your part in a specific way, and ask what they need. "I hated how that went. I'm sorry I raised my voice. Can we try again?" does more than any perfect argument.

First, get the timing right

Trying to make up while either of you is still flooded usually turns into round two. If you need time, say so, and say when you'll be back. Silence with no end date feels like punishment, even when it isn't meant that way.

  • "I need a little time to cool down, but I'm not going anywhere. Can we talk after dinner?"
  • "I don't want to keep fighting. I'll come find you in half an hour."

The three parts of a good apology

1. Acknowledge their feelings

Not whether they were right, just that what they felt makes sense from where they stood. This is often the thing they were fighting to get.

2. Own your part, specifically

"I'm sorry for everything" is vague and easy to dismiss. "I'm sorry I brought up your mum" is real. Owning one specific thing doesn't mean you agree with everything they said.

3. Ask what they need

End with a question, not a closing statement. It hands them a way back into the conversation without having to give in.

What to say: examples

These work whether it's your boyfriend, your girlfriend, your husband or your wife. Change the words until they sound like you; a sentence that doesn't sound like you won't land.

  • "I hated how that went. I'm sorry I raised my voice."Acknowledges the moment and owns one thing.
  • "I can see why that hurt. I'd have felt the same."Validates without debating the facts.
  • "I got defensive instead of listening. Can you tell me again what you meant?"Owns your part and reopens the door.
  • "I care more about us than about being right about this."Lowers the stakes for both of you.
  • "What would help right now: talking it through, or a hug and talking tomorrow?"Lets them choose how to reconnect.
  • "I don't think either of us said what we really meant. Can we try again, slower?"Shares the blame for how it went, not for what was said.
  • "Next time I feel that wound up, I'm going to ask for a break instead of snapping."Names one change you'll actually make.

What to text after a fight

Text is fine for opening the door, and bad for having the whole conversation. Keep it short, warm and about reconnecting, then talk properly in person or on a call.

  • "I'm sorry about earlier. I don't like it when we're like this. Can we talk tonight?"
  • "I've been thinking about what you said, and I think I get it now. I'd like to hear it again, properly."
  • "Still a bit upset, but I love you. Can we have a proper talk later?"
  • "I'm sorry for what I said about your friends. That wasn't fair."

What to avoid over text: long paragraphs re-arguing your case, sarcasm (it never reads as intended), and sending several messages in a row while they're quiet.

Things that restart the fight

  • "I'm sorry you feel that way."Apologises for their feelings, not your actions.
  • "I'm sorry, but…"Everything after "but" cancels the apology.
  • "Can we just forget it happened?"Feels like being asked to drop something that still hurts.
  • "You always do this."Turns one moment into a character judgement.
  • "Fine." or "Whatever you want."Sounds like agreement, lands as withdrawal.

If they're not ready to talk

Let them have the space, and make it clear the door is open. One kind message, then wait. Chasing them for a reply usually pushes the conversation further away.

  • "I'm here when you're ready. No rush. I love you."

If you were the one who got hurt

You're allowed to say what you need instead of waiting for the perfect apology. Be specific and keep it about this moment.

  • "I'm not looking for you to say I was right. I just need to hear that you get why that hurt."

Then come back to the real issue

Making up isn't the same as solving. Once you're both calm, come back to what the argument was really about, usually a need underneath the topic, and agree on one small step. If it's an argument you keep having, our guide on how to stop fighting with your partner walks through how.

Repair shouldn't be one-way. If you're always the one apologising, or you're afraid of how your partner will react, that's worth talking to someone about. If you feel unsafe, contact local emergency services or a qualified professional.

Common questions

How long should you wait to talk after a fight?

Long enough for both of you to feel calm, usually at least twenty minutes and often a few hours. Waiting days without saying when you'll talk tends to feel like punishment, so name a time.

Should I text after a fight?

A short, warm text to open the door is fine: say you're sorry about how it went and ask to talk later. Have the actual conversation in person or on a call, where tone doesn't get lost.

Who should apologise first?

Whoever is ready first. Apologising for your part isn't admitting the whole fight was your fault, and it usually makes it easier for the other person to own theirs.

What if they don't accept my apology?

Give them time and don't argue about whether the apology was good enough. Let them know you meant it and that you're ready to talk when they are.
